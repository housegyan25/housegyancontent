What a west facing plan shows

A clear plan marks living areas, bedrooms, kitchen, bathrooms, and balconies. In a west facing plan the living room can be placed to avoid harsh afternoon sun. Windows and overhangs are shown to reduce heat. The plan also shows paths for wind to pass through the house. This helps keep rooms fresh and lowers the need for fans.

Steps to pick the right layout

Start by noting your plot size and which way it faces. Think about how your family uses rooms in the day and evening. Place private bedrooms away from the road. Keep the main living area in a place that gets light but not too much hot sun in the afternoon. Put the kitchen near plumbing lines and with a window for smoke and heat to leave the house. If you want a small prayer area, keep it in a quiet corner.

Design tips for comfort

Use shaded balconies and overhangs to block strong afternoon sun. Add trees and climbing plants on the west side to reduce heat. Place windows for cross ventilation to let air flow in the evening. Use light colors for flooring and walls to reflect heat. Built in storage saves floor space and keeps rooms tidy. Plan sockets and lights where you will place furniture and electronics.

Materials and budget tips

A good plan lets you list main materials like bricks, cement, tiles, and paint. Ask your builder for an estimate using the drawing. Check local prices and add a small buffer for changes. Choosing common tile sizes and standard door sizes can save money. Compare quotes from two or three builders before you start.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not change room sizes after work starts. Keep windows and vents as per the plan to keep airflow. Avoid placing the kitchen where smoke can go into living spaces. Do not forget to mark electrical points and plumbing lines on the drawing. These small checks save time on site.

Before you build

Share the final drawing with your builder and ask for a quote. Keep a printed copy on site so workers can follow the plan. Update the drawing if you need changes before construction starts. Use the plan for local permits and checks. A final approved drawing keeps work smooth and avoids costly delays.
